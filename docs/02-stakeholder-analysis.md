# Stakeholder Analysis

## Purpose

This stakeholder analysis identifies the people and organizations that influence, implement, or are affected by the Peachtree Logistics Group Security Risk and IAM Program. The analysis distinguishes formal decision-making authority from practical influence over whether controls work in daily operations.

## Stakeholder Map

| Stakeholder | Role in Project | Influence | Primary Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chief Operating Officer | Executive sponsor; approves priorities and resources | High | Maintaining reliable operations while reducing risk |
| Chief Information Officer / IT Director | Owns technology infrastructure; approves and implements technical controls | High | Balancing security improvements with system stability and IT resource constraints |
| GRC Analyst (Project Lead) | Conducts the assessment, facilitates interviews, and develops findings and recommendations | Medium | Producing an accurate, actionable, and realistic risk picture |
| Human Resources Manager | Provides insight into onboarding, offboarding, personnel policies, and workforce structure | Medium | Ensuring recommended controls are enforceable and fair across the workforce |
| Warehouse Operations Manager | Represents warehouse workflows and validates the operational impact of proposed controls | Medium | Keeping fulfillment and inventory processes moving without unnecessary friction |
| Dispatch Manager | Represents routing and driver-coordination workflows and validates effects on time-sensitive dispatch | Medium | Ensuring controls do not delay route assignments or deliveries |
| Medical Courier Program Manager | Oversees medical-delivery operations and supports assessment of PHI/ePHI handling practices | Medium | Protecting applicable PHI/ePHI while meeting strict delivery windows for medical clients |
| Drivers and Couriers | Use mobile devices and delivery applications and execute delivery and security procedures in the field | Low formal / High practical | Controls that remain safe and workable during time-sensitive mobile operations |
| Employees and Warehouse Personnel | Use office and warehouse systems and follow daily security practices | Low formal / High practical | Security measures that do not create unnecessary barriers to completing assigned work |
| Vendor Management / Procurement | Manages vendor contracts, onboarding, data-sharing agreements, and offboarding | Medium | Ensuring vendor relationships meet security and compliance expectations without disrupting sourcing |
| Third-Party Technology Vendors | Provide logistics, freight-brokerage, and supporting technology services | Low formal / Medium practical | Meeting PLG's requirements while supporting secure integrations, logging, authentication, and configuration changes |
| Healthcare and Commercial Clients | Rely on PLG for reliable and secure delivery services and may impose contractual requirements | Medium contractual | Confidence that shipments and information, including applicable PHI/ePHI, are handled securely |

## Stakeholder Engagement Approach

| Stakeholder Group | Engagement Method | Project Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Executive leadership | Sponsor interviews, risk briefings, and decision meetings | Initiation, prioritization, and final approval |
| IT and system owners | Technical interviews, evidence requests, and control-design workshops | Discovery, assessment, and remediation planning |
| HR, procurement, and compliance | Process walkthroughs and policy reviews | Discovery and control design |
| Operations, warehouse, and dispatch managers | Workflow observations, interviews, and recommendation validation | Discovery, human-factors analysis, and implementation planning |
| Drivers, couriers, and frontline personnel | Short interviews, workflow observations, and usability feedback | Discovery, awareness design, and control testing |
| Vendors and clients | Contract and evidence review through PLG relationship owners | Third-party and contractual-risk assessment |

## Human-Centered Governance Considerations

- Formal authority does not always predict whether a control will succeed in practice.
- Drivers, couriers, dispatchers, and warehouse personnel have substantial practical influence because they apply controls during time-sensitive work.
- Controls should be tested at safe workflow points, including dispatch, pickup, parked status updates, and delivery confirmation. Drivers should never be expected to interact with applications while actively driving.
- Leadership owns risk acceptance and funding decisions; the GRC Analyst develops and communicates the evidence-based recommendations.
- Independent couriers require separate access, contractual, device-security, monitoring, and offboarding considerations because they operate outside PLG's internal trust boundary.
