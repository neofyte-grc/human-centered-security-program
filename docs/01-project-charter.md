Peachtree Logistics Group Security Risk and IAM Program

Project type: Fictional GRC portfolio case study
Organization: Peachtree Logistics Group (PLG)
Location: Atlanta, Georgia
Project lead: Tommy Marshall, GRC Analyst

Company Overview

Peachtree Logistics Group (PLG) is a regional logistics company headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. The company employs approximately 225 people and provides warehousing, last-mile delivery, medical courier, and freight brokerage services throughout the Southeastern United States.

Its workforce includes corporate employees, dispatchers, warehouse personnel, drivers, contractors, managers, IT personnel, and third-party vendors. These teams are supported by a mixed technology environment consisting of cloud platforms, mobile devices, on-premises servers, warehouse systems, and logistics applications. During daily operations, PLG collects, processes, and stores employee records, customer information, delivery records, medical-delivery information, payment data, contracts, and vendor information.

PLG initiated this security program following increased phishing attempts, inconsistent access-control practices, and new customer security requirements. The program must strengthen security without introducing controls that prevent employees, drivers, and operational teams from completing time-sensitive work.

Problem Statement

PLG currently lacks consistent identity and access management (IAM) controls across its mixed cloud and on-premises environment. This weakness increases the risk of account compromise, unauthorized system access, and exposure of personally identifiable information (PII) and sensitive delivery data. A security incident could disrupt time-sensitive logistics operations, damage customer trust, and create contractual or regulatory consequences. PLG must identify and prioritize its security risks while implementing controls that employees can realistically follow without unnecessarily interrupting their normal workflows.

Project Goal

The goal of this project is to identify and assess security risks across PLG's people, processes, technology, facilities, and third-party relationships. The resulting security program will reduce organizational risk while supporting the natural workflows and time-sensitive responsibilities of PLG's workforce.

Project Objectives

Identify and assess security risks involving PLG's people, business processes, technology systems, facilities, and third-party relationships.

Evaluate existing IAM practices and recommend role-based access controls, including standardized access approval, provisioning, modification, review, and termination procedures.

Develop practical security controls and awareness materials based on how employees perform their daily responsibilities, minimizing unnecessary disruption to operational workflows and time-sensitive services.

Establish key performance indicators (KPIs) and key risk indicators (KRIs) to evaluate control performance under realistic conditions and quantify meaningful changes in organizational risk.

Scope

In Scope

Microsoft 365 environment — Email, SharePoint, Teams, OneDrive, and associated identity and access configurations.

Cloud logistics and freight-brokerage applications — Core SaaS platforms used to manage loads, dispatch, and carrier relationships.

Warehouse management systems — Inventory and fulfillment software used at PLG facilities.

On-premises servers and network infrastructure — Locally hosted servers, network equipment, directory services, file storage, and connections between PLG facilities and cloud platforms.

Employee laptops and mobile devices — Corporate-issued endpoints used by office and warehouse personnel.

Driver mobile devices and delivery applications — PLG applications, company information, and access controls used by drivers on PLG-issued or approved personal devices. Vehicle mechanical and telematics systems remain excluded.

User accounts and access permissions — Identity and access management practices across all in-scope systems.

Warehouses and corporate offices — Physical security practices at PLG-operated locations, including badge access, visitor logs, and secured storage areas.

Employee security practices — How personnel and drivers handle credentials, company information, and daily security-relevant tasks.

Medical-delivery information — Handling of medical-delivery information, including any PHI/ePHI received from healthcare clients.

Payment and customer information — How customer PII, billing information, and any applicable payment-card information are stored, transmitted, and accessed.

Contractors and third-party vendors — PLG's vendor access provisioning, data-sharing practices, contractual requirements, and offboarding processes. This is a review of PLG's management of those relationships, not an assessment of vendor-owned environments.

Out of Scope

Vehicle mechanical systems — Engines, telematics hardware, and onboard diagnostics managed as fleet-maintenance concerns.

Vendor-owned internal networks — Infrastructure PLG does not directly control or operate.

Physical building construction — Structural, electrical, and architectural elements, as opposed to security practices conducted inside PLG facilities.

Full penetration testing — Active exploitation of production systems; this engagement is a risk and control assessment rather than an offensive-security test.

Assumptions

PLG leadership and department managers will make themselves available for interviews and provide candid, accurate information about current practices.

PLG will provide all available system inventories, access logs, and identity documentation. Missing, incomplete, or informally maintained documentation will be recorded as an evidence gap and considered when evaluating control maturity.

Employees and drivers use a mixture of PLG-issued and approved personal mobile devices to perform their duties, and both will be considered during the assessment.

No active security breach or incident is known to PLG when the assessment begins; findings will therefore reflect a baseline risk posture rather than an incident-response investigation.

Constraints

The assessment must be conducted without interrupting warehouse operations or time-sensitive delivery and medical-courier services.

Production systems cannot be actively exploited or penetration-tested. Findings will be based on interviews, documentation review, and configuration and control review.

PLG operates with limited dedicated security personnel and budget, constraining the complexity and cost of recommended controls.

Vendor and third-party environments cannot be directly inspected. Vendor risk will be evaluated through PLG's contracts, access controls, data-sharing practices, and relationship-management processes.

Executive summary

Success Criteria

The project will be successful when PLG has a prioritized and evidence-supported view of its security risks, practical IAM and security-control recommendations, clearly assigned responsibilities, and measurable indicators that demonstrate whether controls reduce risk without creating unreasonable operational friction.

Portfolio Disclaimer

Peachtree Logistics Group is a fictional organization created for educational and portfolio purposes. The scenario, systems, risks, and recommendations do not describe an actual company or security engagement.
